## Tessellation: historical wedges and folds around rounded corners

**Status:** historical geometry folds not reproduced in the inspected current cobblestone/fort views; the October 1 laboratory stone triangles are separately reproduced and isolated to contact-shadow shading below; original-engine parity remains unverified. **Severity:** visual. The old `(15, 0, 24)` location points toward the dragon courtyard, not the fort cannon. Current cannon inspection uses camera `(-131, -89, 23)`, yaw `210°`, pitch `-24°`; authored tour segment 16 begins at 152.7 seconds.

An earlier loader displaced ordinary POM-only materials whenever it found a height or density texture and supplied invented displacement amplitudes. That incorrectly warped surfaces that the original engine left geometrically flat. Material-class gating removed that cause, but the old investigation did not establish that every reported wedge disappeared. Some historical examples also persisted with tessellation disabled; do not assume that every triangular artifact has the same cause.

### Current implementation

[scene_rhi.cpp](../benchmark/src/engine/scene_rhi.cpp) requires an authored tessellation material and positive `tessellation_length` for ordinary topology subdivision. Explicit surface policies for enhanced island and village ground are separate exceptions. The `_t` map controls neutral CPU topology; `_h` supplies height. Material UV transforms apply to the corresponding sampling paths. `tessellation_length` controls target edge length, while the independently authored displacement scale is carried through [material_rhi.cpp](../benchmark/src/engine/material_rhi.cpp) and [material_instance_cache.cpp](../benchmark/src/rhi/material_instance_cache.cpp).

The CPU produces subdivided rest geometry. Material vertex shaders, including [heaven_lit.mat](../benchmark/materials/heaven_lit.mat), apply camera-dependent height displacement with the authored scale, power, distance threshold/range and height mip selection. Consequently, inspecting cached CPU vertices alone does not show the final displaced silhouette. [cpu_tessellation.cpp](../benchmark/src/engine/cpu_tessellation.cpp) shares indexed edge midpoints and propagates subdivision splits within a surface; optional boundary fade belongs to the selected surface policy. It does not universally pin every material boundary.

The scene loader uses ZIP cache semantic version **36**, `kTessellationArchiveVersion` in [tessellation_cache_archive.h](../benchmark/src/engine/tessellation_cache_archive.h), which is also printed at startup. The loose-cache helper uses semantic version **34**, `kTessellationCacheVersion` in [cpu_tessellation.h](../benchmark/src/engine/cpu_tessellation.h). The archive importer accepts only current `v34` loose entries whose directory, JSON version and binary-header version agree; it cannot re-key obsolete `v33` geometry into a new ZIP. Cache keys include source fingerprints and topology parameters. Earlier references to versions 12 or 21, a `sample_height_smooth` 3×3 kernel, and CPU-baked final displacement describe older implementations. `HEAVEN_DUMP_TESS` is not an active diagnostic option.

The version bump invalidates neutral geometry generated by the old subtraction-based midpoint interpolation. Oppositely traversed copies of an otherwise identical roof edge rounded to different position bits, preventing exact position/UV footprint sharing and producing different displaced heights. [cpu_tessellation.cpp](../benchmark/src/engine/cpu_tessellation.cpp) now uses order-independent POSITION/UV0/UV1 midpoints while preserving raw-normal and tangent interpolation, separate tangent-seam vertices and topology. The reverse-duplicate-edge native regression passes with the correction and fails when the old interpolation is restored. This fixes an independently demonstrated seam inconsistency; it does not establish removal of VA-23's separate foreground contact-shadow boundary.

The remaining folding hypothesis is that nearby vertices with different smooth normals can displace along diverging directions. This has not been demonstrated on the current GPU displacement path. Face-normal displacement, amplitude clamping or smoothing would change the image and need fresh evidence before implementation.

### Current implementation

[MaterialInstanceCache](../benchmark/src/rhi/material_instance_cache.cpp) routes matching window materials and inherited window variants through the masked, two-sided [heaven_window.mat](../benchmark/materials/heaven_window.mat). Classification uses the xml0-derived material name and parent chain through [window_material_ref.h](../benchmark/src/engine/window_material_ref.h). The window shader preserves the diffuse cutout, separates glass-like pane response from the frame, and applies a capped, desaturated warm emission ramp. Its source emission texture remains `heaven/textures/buildings/building_windows_e.dds` where authored. The generic `heaven_lit` shader samples emission RGB and multiplies it by tint and a luminance mask; the old description of a current fixed-color replacement is inaccurate.

[post_process.cpp](../benchmark/src/rhi/post_process.cpp) configures TAA; the backend disables FXAA. [LightingSystem](../benchmark/src/rhi/lighting_system.cpp) retunes bloom for time of day and uses HIGH quality to avoid coarse emissive halos. The old suggestions to first disable active FXAA or inspect a MEDIUM-only bloom setup no longer describe the default code. Tone mapping, sampled emission mips, temporal filtering and material classification remain legitimate comparison points if the artifact can be reproduced.

The audit captures a close cottage window at `(-23.34942, 29.89840, 22.34291)`, yaw `-48.12304°`, pitch `0°`, at midnight and 21:00, including a 30° FOV closeup and bloom on/off. This particular window has closed louvres. Follow-up cameras at `(-16.55995, 28.88356, 22.25873)`, yaw `-15.58437°`, and `(-15.51666, 18.35748, 25.45067)`, yaw `131.87696°`, both at pitch `0°` and 21:00, show unobstructed open-shutter windows on house2 and house1. The wider 45° images appear smoothly warm, but the house2 30° view reveals stepped blocks above and below the pane (VA-18). They remain with bloom, SSR, SSGI, DOF, TAA, AO or point-light shadows individually disabled. The original 1024² BC1 emission map contains a baked halo with compression steps, and the shader's smoothstep mask accentuates its contours. Linear/trilinear sampling is configured correctly. This supports an asset/mask limitation, while exact original-engine comparison remains unavailable. Use actual pass-disable controls; reducing quality is not equivalent to disabling a pass.

## Night sky, clouds, fog and HDR grain

**Status:** confirmed rendering defects addressed; evidence is in [VISUAL_AUDIT.md](VISUAL_AUDIT.md). Night stars and moon emission were incorrectly multiplied by the daytime lux conversion. Volumetric clouds retained a fixed white contribution after sunset, and volumetric fog used daytime ambient light at midnight while skipping the sky. Together these produced black skies, white cloud banks and pale, untextured distant silhouettes. Night sky emission now retains its scene-linear units, cloud illumination follows the existing sun/moon policy, and fog integrates the same finite atmospheric volume for geometry and sky with hour-dependent illumination.

The HDR path also applied an 8-bit dithering amplitude to linear floating-point color before PQ encoding. This amplified near-black grain. Disabling that stage for HDR removes the grain in the recorded PQ-derived preview; SDR keeps its existing output dithering. The capture retains raw PQ floats and does not measure physical monitor brightness.

The optional legacy cloud deck had incorrect local/world ray coordinates and no geometry depth test, causing foreground whitening. Its corrected rays and far-plane depth-tested quad let foreground geometry occlude the clouds. It remains disabled by default and is distinct from volumetric clouds. The audit uses the complete 410-entry asset pack containing all three legacy cloud textures; older packs can skip the feature and produce misleading on/off comparisons.

## Grass color and close-camera height

**Status:** albedo clipping fixed; blade-height appearance remains open. The old shader implemented its documented ±25% variation by adding an offset to low linear texture values, clipping 69.5% of evaluated base samples completely black. It now multiplies albedo by the bounded variation factor, preserving lighting, normals, shadows and geometry. Actual before/after views are retained for regular and massive foliage at `68.9` seconds.

## Lens-flare distance fading

**Status:** coordinate mismatch fixed. The shader compared a camera-shifted vertex position with the unshifted camera position, so nearby lamps could fade out merely because the camera was far from the scene origin. It now uses Filament's camera position in the same coordinate system. The regression renders the actual feature and embedded material, checks matching pixels after translating camera and lamp together, and checks that truly distant lamps still fade. A port-lamp closeup supplies actual-scene on/off coverage.

## Contact-shadow flicker and triangular stone patches

**Status:** the reported bright wall triangles and stippled bevel were corrected in the contact-depth sampler; this does not close the separate October 3 roof defect above. See [the wall audit](VISUAL_AUDIT.md#rounded-stone-contact-shadow-regression). The large bright criss-cross pattern at camera `(-28,1,23)`, yaw 60°, pitch 0°, FOV 50° is a regression from our `ec8b818` receiver-plane rejection. It disappears with the preceding `b0b650a` packages or contact shadows disabled and persists with tessellation disabled. This specific report is a shading defect, not demonstrated holes in the displaced mesh or a consequence of low FPS.

The corrected shader retains the unjittered depth projection, removes per-triangle rejection/origin offsets, and checks both reconstructed bilinear depth and nearest depth. That avoids nearest-texel staircases without treating separated objects as one interpolated surface. A ray entering its own receiver is immediately occluded; this also removes the smaller stippled bevel where a dithered first step could jump beyond the depth slab. The curved-surface GPU regression fails with the defective packages. It also corrects the previous test's mistaken assumption that a light traveling below a receiver's geometry must leave it illuminated. Contact shadows and geometric self-shadowing remain enabled; rounded stone bevels and grazing cottage ledges can therefore be darker than the erroneous plane-rejection output.

The original receiver activation in `38df114` exposed the earlier flicker; the subsequent `ec8b818` introduced this distinct triangulation regression. Full-resolution structure depth remains required whenever contact shadows are enabled, including with AO off. Historical flat-plane and wide-scene validation did not cover the reported close curved surface. Other screen-space, silhouette and foliage sampling limitations remain; this correction is not a claim that every possible scene is free of temporal artifacts.
